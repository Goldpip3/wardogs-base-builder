FMJ The baseline every other load is measured against, and the cheapest thing in the vendor list. Armour takes a serious but survivable share.

HP Ruined by armour. A level 4 vest takes better than ninety-nine percent of it, which is as close to nothing as this game gets. Against a bare zone it is meant to be the heavy hitter, and that figure is the one hole in this page.

AP Barely notices armour: two thirds of it still lands through level 4. It is also the most expensive thing on the shelf, and against a bare zone it does nothing standard would not.

Buckshot Eight pellets, each carrying an eighth of the hit, and they can miss separately. Every shotgun figure on this page assumes all eight land unless you say otherwise.

Slug One heavy projectile. Armour treats it exactly as it treats standard, so the difference is all in what is behind it.

How a shot resolves

Four things happen, in this order, and nothing else does. Knowing the order is most of knowing the game.

1. The weapon

Every weapon has one damage figure, measured at the upper torso at point blank. Two rifles in the same calibre are not necessarily the same weapon: an M4 and a Galil both fire 5.56 and one of them hits harder. That figure is derived, not copied, and hovering one on this page shows the interval it was solved out of.

2. Where it lands

The zone multiplies it. A head is 2.17 times an upper torso, a hand is 0.27. The multiplier belongs to the body and not to the gun, which is why one profile covers every weapon, and it is measured off the assault rifle column with the caveat noted below.

3. What is covering it

Armour only counts on the zones it covers. A helmet covers the head. A vest covers the three torso zones. Nothing covers the neck, the pelvis, the arms or the legs, so a level 4 vest changes precisely nothing about a shot to the hip. The hatching on the figure above is the armour you have set, drawn where it actually is.

4. Penetration is the load

There is no separate penetration stat. What gets through is decided by which of the five loads you fired and which tier it hit, and that is the whole of it. Armour piercing keeps two thirds of itself through level 4. Flesh damage keeps three quarters of one percent. Standard sits between them and costs a quarter of what AP does.

Flesh damage, honestly

HP is the round this page is least sure about. Its armour figures are published and used here. Its damage against a bare zone is not published anywhere, so the calculator treats it as equal to standard, which is a floor and almost certainly low: the vendor charges nearly twice as much for it. Do not read an unarmoured HP number here as a measurement.

Buckshot is eight things

A 12g shell is eight pellets, each carrying an eighth of the hit, and they spread. Every shotgun figure here assumes all eight land, which is true at a doorway and a fantasy at thirty metres. The pellet slider in the calculator is there so you can be honest with yourself.

Three things on this page are missing on purpose

  • What flesh damage actually does to flesh. Every armour figure for HP is published and this page uses them, but no source gives its damage against an unarmoured zone, so the calculator treats it as equal to standard there. That is almost certainly wrong: the vendor charges nearly twice as much for it, and a round that was strictly worse everywhere would not sell. Read every unarmoured HP figure on this page as a floor rather than a value.
  • Range falloff. Damage clearly drops with distance and slower rounds drop faster, but no published table separates falloff from the other factors, so every damage figure here is point blank. Flight time is shown because muzzle velocity is published. Damage at range is not shown, because it is not.
  • Zone multipliers outside the torso are measured off the 5.56 assault rifle profile. The head, neck and limb ratios hold across four weapons; the torso ratio does not, and varies by up to a fifth. Non-torso figures for heavier calibres are indicative until launch.

Health is 100. Per-weapon damage is solved back out of published shots-to-kill figures rather than copied, which means it arrives with error bars: hover a damage figure to see the interval it came from. The full working, and the checks it has to pass on every build, are written up in docs/ballistics-sources.md in the repository.
